A mother’s worst fear, a team’s finest hour: Saving baby Xavier

For two-month-old Xavier Hosking, what began as a common childhood illness quickly became a life-threatening emergency, requiring a coordinated response from South Australia’s specialist emergency care teams.

Xavier

As an infant, Xavier faced a serious battle with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in his hometown of Port Lincoln.

“I remember looking at Xavier and he just had no colour, and he was not his normal self,” Xavier’s mother Mandy said.

“He was not feeding and his breathing had become worse.”

RSV

RSV is highly dangerous for babies, particularly those under six months, as it can cause severe lower respiratory tract infections like bronchiolitis and pneumonia.

Because infants have narrow airways and developing immune systems, they frequently require hospitalisation and oxygen support when infected.

Oxygen support
Distance

For families in regional areas, distance can add another layer of complexity. While Port Lincoln is a seven-hour drive from Adelaide, it is less than an hour away by air – a critical difference when every minute counts.

Response

When Xavier's condition worsened, a network of local clinicians, the Royal Flying Doctor Service SA/NT (RFDS) and SA Ambulance (SAAS) MedSTAR worked together to coordinate his transfer to Adelaide for life-saving treatment.

“As his condition deteriorated rapidly, local clinicians had no choice but to insert a nasogastric tube and oxygen, at which stage we had to be flown,” Mandy, Xavier's mother.

“The SAAS MedSTAR and RFDS crew arrived and we were so looked after – from the flight to the road ambulance in Adelaide to the hospital.”

The network

Tasked with caring for Xavier, SAAS MedSTAR Paediatric Retrieval Consultant Dr Bron Hennebry said strong collaboration between regional clinicians, retrieval specialists and aeromedical crews is essential to ensuring critically ill children receive specialist care as quickly as possible.

The team
“Patients don’t see two separate organisations – they see a team. That’s exactly how it should be. The partnership means patients like Xavier receive seamless care, from the moment they’re stabilised, through to transfer and treatment,”
Flight Nurse Manager Ben Poppit.
Today

Today, Xavier is a thriving three-year-old, thanks to the quick response of our vital health chain.

Looking back, Mandy knows how fortunate her family was to have that network of care when they needed it most.

“Knowing there was a whole network of people working together to care for Xavier gave me such peace of mind. The feeling of being supported is so special when you live in a regional community,” Mandy, Xavier's mother.
